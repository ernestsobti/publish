### Ratio 

> A ratio is a comparison of the sizes of two or more quantities of the same kind by division.

> If $a$ and $b$ are quantities of the same kind (in same units), then the fraction $a/b$ is called the ratio of $a$ to $b$.  

> It is written as $a : b$.

The quantities $a$ and $b$ are called the terms of the ratio.  
- $a$ is called the <u>first term or antecedent</u>.  
- $b$ is called the <u>second term or consequent</u>.



#### Features of Ratio 

> [!info]- Both terms of a ratio can be multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number.  
> The value of the ratio does not change.
> Example:  
> $4 : 5$  
> Multiplying both terms by $3$ gives $12 : 15$  
> 
> Also,
> $\frac{4}{5} = 0.8$
> 
> Usually, a ratio is expressed in lowest terms (simplest form).

> [!info] The  <u>order of the terms</u> in a ratio is important 




> [!info]- Ratio exists only between quantities of the same kind.  
> 
> Example:  
> Find ratio between 150 g and 2 kg.
> 
> Convert to same units:  
> $150\text{ g}$ and $2000\text{ g}$
> 
> Ratio  
> $$
> 150 : 2000 = 3 : 40
> $$
> 
> Similarly, time must be in same units  
> (e.g. minutes and seconds converted before forming ratio).



> [!Info]- To Compare two ratios, convert them into equivalent frations
> Example: compare which ratio is greater  
> $2\frac{1}{3} : 3\frac{1}{3}$ and $3.6 : 4.8$
> 
> Convert to fractions  
> $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$ , $3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$  
> 
> So the ratio  
> $\frac{7}{3} : \frac{10}{3} = 7 : 10$
> 
> Also,  
> $3.6 : 4.8 = 36 : 48 = 3 : 4$
> 
> Compare fractions  
> $\frac{7}{10}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$
> 
> Since $\frac{3}{4} > \frac{7}{10}$,  
> $3.6 : 4.8$ is the greater ratio.
> 


> [!info]- If a quantity increases or decreases in the ratio $a : b$,
> 
> $$
> \text{new quantity} = \frac{b}{a} \times \text{original quantity}
> $$
> 
> The fraction $\frac{b}{a}$ by which the original quantity is multiplied to get the new quantity is called the <u>factor multiplying ratio.</u> [[#^d71917|Illustration]]
> 





#### Properties of Ratio 



> [!info] Inverse Ratio
> One ratio is the inverse of another if their product is $1$.  
> If $a : b$ is a ratio, then its inverse ratio is $b : a$ (and vice versa). [[#^68276b|Illustration]]
> 


> [!info] Compounded Ratio 
> The ratio compounded of two ratios $a : b$ and $c : d$ is $ac : bd$.
> 
> Compounding two or more ratios means multiplying them. [[#^2f13c3|Illustration]]
> 
> 




> [!info] Duplicate ratio and triplicate ratio
> A ratio compounded of itself is called its duplicate ratio.  
> If the ratio is $a : b$, its duplicate ratio is $a^2 : b^2$.
> 
> Example:  
> If $a : b = 2 : 3$,  
> duplicate ratio $= 2^2 : 3^2 = 4 : 9$.
> 
> The triplicate ratio of $a : b$ is $a^3 : b^3$.


> [!info] Sub-duplicate and sub-triplicate ratio
> 
> The sub-duplicate ratio of $a : b$ is $\sqrt{a} : \sqrt{b}$.
> 
> The sub-triplicate ratio of $a : b$ is $\sqrt[3]{a} : \sqrt[3]{b}$.
> 



> [!info] Commensurable and incommensurable quantities
> If the ratio of two similar quantities can be expressed as a ratio of two integers, the quantities are called commensurable.
> 
> Otherwise, they are called incommensurable.
> 
> Example of incommensurable quantities:  
> $\sqrt{3} : \sqrt{2}$




> [!info] Continued ratio
> Continued ratio is the comparison between the magnitudes of three or more quantities of the same kind.
> 
> If the quantities are $a$, $b$, and $c$, the continued ratio is written as $a : b : c$.
> 
> Example:  
> ₹200 : ₹400 : ₹600 = $1 : 2 : 3$

> [!Question] If $A : B = 6 : 7$ and $B : C = 3 : 4$, find $A : B : C$.
> 
> Write both ratios with common term $B$.
> 
> $A : B = 6 : 7$  
> $B : C = 3 : 4$
> 
> Make $B$ same in both ratios.  
> LCM of $7$ and $3$ is $21$.
> 
> So,
> $A : B = 18 : 21$  
> $B : C = 21 : 28$
> 
> Therefore,
> $A : B : C = 18 : 21 : 28$

> [!Question]- If Kapil’s weight increases in the ratio $8 : 11$ and his current weight is $56$
> 
> Original : New = $8 : 11$
> 
> $$
> \frac{\text{original weight}}{\text{new weight}} = \frac{8}{11}
> $$
> 
> $$
> \frac{56}{\text{new weight}} = \frac{8}{11}
> $$
> 
> $$
> \text{new weight} = \frac{56 \times 11}{8} = 77
> $$
> 
> New weight = $77$
> 
> Shortcut (factor multiplying ratio):
> 
> Increase in ratio $a : b \Rightarrow$ multiply by $\frac{b}{a}$
> 
> $$
> 56 \times \frac{11}{8} = 77
> $$

> [!Question]- If $A : B = 7 : 10$ and $C : A = 9 : 14$, find $A : B : C$.
> 
> Write both ratios with common term $A$.
> 
> $A : B = 7 : 10$  
> $C : A = 9 : 14$
> 
> Make $A$ same in both ratios.  
> LCM of $7$ and $14$ is $14$.
> 
> So,
> $A : B = 14 : 20$  
> $C : A = 9 : 14$
> 
> Therefore,
> $A : B : C = 14 : 20 : 9$


> [!Tip]- Shortcut (fast MCQ method):
> Given  
> $A : B = 7 : 10$  
> $C : A = 9 : 14$
> 
> Step 1: Make $A$ common mentally  
> From $C : A = 9 : 14 \Rightarrow A = 14$ parts  
> From $A : B = 7 : 10 \Rightarrow A = 7$ parts  
> 
> Step 2: Scale $A : B$ so that $A = 14$  
> Multiply by $2$  
> 
> $A : B = 14 : 20$
> 
> Step 3: Read $C$ directly  
> From $C : A = 9 : 14 \Rightarrow C = 9$
> 
> Answer  
> $A : B : C = 14 : 20 : 9$



## Problem Set 

### Compounded Ratio 

> [!Question]- The ratio compounded of $4 : 9$ and the duplicate ratio of $3 : 4$ is
> 
> Standard method:
> 
> Duplicate ratio of $3 : 4$  
> $$
> 3^2 : 4^2 = 9 : 16
> $$
> 
> Compound the ratios:
> $$
> \frac{4}{9} \times \frac{9}{16}
> $$
> 
> Simplifying,
> $$
> = \frac{1}{4}
> $$
> 
> Compounded ratio = $1 : 4$
> 
> Alternate fast method (MCQ):
> 
> Duplicate of $3 : 4$ is $9 : 16$.  
> Cancel $9$ immediately with denominator of $4 : 9$.  
> Remaining ratio $4 : 16 = 1 : 4$.

> [!example] Illustration
> P, Q and R are three cities. The ratio of average temperature between P and Q is $11 : 12$ and that between P and R is $9 : 8$. Find the ratio between the average temperature of Q and R.


$$
\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3^2}{4^2}
= \frac{4 \times 9}{9 \times 16}
= \frac{1}{4}
= 1 : 4
$$
