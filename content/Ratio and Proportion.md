## Ratio 

### Basic

> A ratio is a comparison of the sizes of two or more quantities of the same kind by division.

> If $a$ and $b$ are quantities of the same kind (in the same units), then the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ is called the ratio of $a$ to $b$.  
> It is written as $a:b$.

The quantities $a$ and $b$ are called the **terms of the ratio**.
- $a$ is the **first term (antecedent)**
- $b$ is the **second term (consequent)**


---

### Features of Ratio

> [!info] Invariance of ratio  
> Both terms of a ratio can be multiplied or divided by the same **non-zero** number without changing the value of the ratio.

> **Example**
> $$
> 4:5 = 12:15
> $$
> since
> $$
> \frac{4}{5}=\frac{12}{15}=0.8
> $$

> Ratios are usually expressed in **lowest terms**.


> [!info] Order matters  
> The order of the terms in a ratio is important.  
> In general,
> $$
> a:b \neq b:a
> $$


> [!info] Same kind, same units  
> A ratio exists only between quantities of the **same kind**.

> **Illustration**
> Find the ratio of $150\text{ g}$ and $2\text{ kg}$.
>
> Convert to same units:
> $$
> 150\text{ g},\;2000\text{ g}
> $$
>
> $$
> 150:2000=3:40
> $$


> [!info] Comparing ratios  
> To compare two ratios, convert them into **equivalent fractions**.

> **Illustration**
>
> Compare $2\frac{1}{3}:3\frac{1}{3}$ and $3.6:4.8$.
>
> $$
> 2\frac{1}{3}=\frac{7}{3},\quad 3\frac{1}{3}=\frac{10}{3}
> $$
>
> $$
> \frac{7}{3}:\frac{10}{3}=7:10
> $$
>
> $$
> 3.6:4.8=36:48=3:4
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> \frac{3}{4}>\frac{7}{10},
> $$
> $3.6:4.8$ is the greater ratio.


> [!info] Change in ratio  
> If a quantity increases or decreases in the ratio $a:b$, then
> $$
> \text{New quantity}=\frac{b}{a}\times\text{Original quantity}
> $$
>
> The fraction $\frac{b}{a}$ is called the **factor multiplying ratio**.


---

### Properties of Ratio

> [!info] Inverse ratio  
> If $a:b$ is a ratio, its inverse ratio is $b:a$.
>
> The product of a ratio and its inverse is $1$.


> [!info] Compounded ratio  
> The compounded ratio of $a:b$ and $c:d$ is
> $$
> ac:bd
> $$
>
> Compounding means **multiplying ratios**.


> [!info] Duplicate and triplicate ratios  
> If the ratio is $a:b$:
>
> - Duplicate ratio: $a^2:b^2$
> - Triplicate ratio: $a^3:b^3$
>
> **Example**
> $$
> 2:3 \Rightarrow 4:9
> $$


> [!info] Sub-duplicate and sub-triplicate ratios  
> - Sub-duplicate ratio: $\sqrt{a}:\sqrt{b}$
> - Sub-triplicate ratio: $\sqrt[3]{a}:\sqrt[3]{b}$


> [!info] Commensurable and incommensurable quantities  
> If the ratio of two similar quantities can be expressed as a ratio of integers, they are **commensurable**.
>
> Otherwise, they are **incommensurable**.
>
> **Example**
> $$
> \sqrt{3}:\sqrt{2}
> $$


> [!info] Continued ratio  
> A continued ratio compares **three or more quantities** of the same kind.
>
> It is written as:
> $$
> a:b:c
> $$
>
> **Example**
> $$
> ₹200:₹400:₹600 = 1:2:3
> $$


> [!note] Illustration — Continued Ratio
>
> If
> $$
> A:B=6:7,\quad B:C=3:4
> $$
>
> Make the common term equal:
> $$
> A:B=18:21,\quad B:C=21:28
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> A:B:C=18:21:28
> $$



## Proportion 

### Basic 

> An equality of two ratios is called a proportion. 



For quantities a,b,c,d are said to be in proportion if 

$$
a:b=c:d 
$$
$$
a:b::c:d
$$


> [!info]
> - The quantities a,b,c,d are called terms of the proportion: a,b,c and d are called its first, second , third and fourth terms respectively. 
> - Terms of proportions can also be called as Proportional
> - First and fourth terms are called extremes (or extreme terms).
> - Second and third terms are called means (or middle terms).


The first two quantities ($a:b$) should be of the same kind and last two quantities ($c:d$) should be of the same kind. 


> [!note] Illustration
>  $₹6 : ₹8 = 12 toffes : 16 toffees$ are in proportion.






### Cross Product Rule 


> if $a:b=c:d$ are in proportion then $ad=bc$ 

$$
\text{Product of extermes} = \text{Prodcut of means}
$$


### Continuous Proportion 

Three quantities $a,b,c$ of the same kind (in same units) are said to be in continuous proportion if $a:b=b:c$ 

$$
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{b}{c}
$$

$$
b^2 = ac
$$

here, 
$a=$ first proportional, 
$c=$ third proportional and 
$b=$ is mean proportional (because $b$ is $GM$ of $a$ and $b$)



> [!info] Geometrical Mean(GM)
> - The geometric mean is the average of a set of products. It is calculated using the products of the terms.
> 
>  - The geometric mean can help analysts, investors, and portfolio managers evaluate the performance of an investment portfolio.
>  
> - By accounting for the effects of compounding, the geometric mean provides more accurate return measures than the arithmetic mean. 
>

### Invertendo

> If $a:b=c:d$, then 

$$
b:a = d:c
$$

> [!note] Illustration
> $$
> \frac{2}{5}=\frac{6}{15}\Rightarrow\frac{5}{2}=\frac{15}{6}
> $$
> $$
> 2:5::6:15\Rightarrow5:2::15:6
> $$


### Alternendo 

>In Alternendo, you compare the alternate terms.  If $a:b=c:d$, then 

$$
a:c=b:d
$$



> [!NOTE] Illustration
> 
> $$
> \frac{2}{5}=\frac{6}{15}\Rightarrow\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{15}
> $$
> 
> $$
> 2:5::6:15\Rightarrow2:6::5:15
> $$




### Componendo

> In Componendo, you add the antecedent and consequent of each ratio.  
> If $a:b=c:d$, then

$$
\frac{a+b}{b}=\frac{c+d}{d}
$$


> [!important] Deriviation : Proof of Componendo
> 
> Start with a proportion:
> $$
> a:b=c:d
> $$
> 
> Rewrite in fraction form:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$
> 
> Add 1 to both sides:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}+1=\frac{c}{d}+1
> $$
> 
> Which gives:
> $$
> \frac{a+b}{b}=\frac{c+d}{d}
> $$


> [!NOTE] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> 2:5 = 6:15
> $$
>
> Apply Componendo:
>
> $$
> \frac{2+5}{5} = \frac{6+15}{15}
> $$
>
> $$
> 7:5 = 21:15
> $$


### Dividendo

> In Dividendo, you subtract the consequent from the antecedent of each ratio.  
> If $a:b=c:d$, then

$$
\frac{a-b}{b}=\frac{c-d}{d}
$$


> [!important] Derivation : Proof of Dividendo
> 
> Start with a proportion:
> $$
> a:b=c:d
> $$
> 
> Rewrite in fraction form:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$
> 
> Subtract 1 from both sides:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}-1=\frac{c}{d}-1
> $$
> 
> Which gives:
> $$
> \frac{a-b}{b}=\frac{c-d}{d}
> $$


> [!NOTE] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> 2:5 = 6:15
> $$
>
> Apply Dividendo:
>
> $$
> \frac{2-5}{5} = \frac{6-15}{15}
> $$
>
> $$
> -3:5 = -9:15
> $$


### Componendo & Dividendo

> In Componendo & Dividendo, you add and subtract the consequent from the antecedent of each ratio.  
> If $a:b=c:d$, then

$$
\frac{a+b}{a-b}=\frac{c+d}{c-d}
$$


> [!important] Derivation : Proof of Componendo & Dividendo
> 
> Start with a proportion:
> $$
> a:b=c:d
> $$
> 
> Rewrite in fraction form:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$
> 
> Add 1 to both sides:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}+1=\frac{c}{d}+1
> $$
> 
> Subtract 1 from both sides:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}-1=\frac{c}{d}-1
> $$
> 
> Divide the two results:
> $$
> \frac{\frac{a}{b}+1}{\frac{a}{b}-1}
> =
> \frac{\frac{c}{d}+1}{\frac{c}{d}-1}
> $$
> 
> Which gives:
> $$
> \frac{a+b}{a-b}=\frac{c+d}{c-d}
> $$


> [!NOTE] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> 2:5 = 6:15
> $$
>
> Apply Componendo & Dividendo:
>
> $$
> \frac{2+5}{2-5} = \frac{6+15}{6-15}
> $$
>
> $$
> 7:(-3) = 21:(-9)
> $$




### Addendo

> In Addendo, you add the corresponding terms of the ratios.  
> If $a:b=c:d$, then

$$
\frac{a+c+e+\ldots}{b+d+f+\ldots}=k
$$



> [!important] Derivation : Proof of Addendo
> 
> Start with a proportion:
> $$
> a:b=c:d
> $$
> 
> Rewrite in fraction form:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$
> 
> Cross-multiply:
> $$
> ad=bc
> $$
> 
> Add $ad$ and $bc$ on both sides:
> $$
> ad+bc=bc+ad
> $$
> 
> Factor both sides:
> $$
> a(d+c)=c(b+d)
> $$
> 
> Divide both sides by $(b+d)(d+c)$:
> $$
> \frac{a+c}{b+d}=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$


> [!NOTE] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> 2:5 = 6:15
> $$
>
> Apply Addendo:
>
> $$
> \frac{2+6}{5+15}
> $$
>
> $$
> 8:20 = 2:5
> $$

### Subtrahendo

> In Subtrahendo, you subtract the corresponding terms of the ratios.  
> If $a:b=c:d$, then

$$
\frac{a-c-e-\ldots}{b-d-f-\ldots}=k
$$



> [!important] Derivation : Proof of Subtrahendo
> 
> Start with a proportion:
> $$
> a:b=c:d
> $$
> 
> Rewrite in fraction form:
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$
> 
> Cross-multiply:
> $$
> ad=bc
> $$
> 
> Subtract $bc$ from both sides:
> $$
> ad-bc=0
> $$
> 
> Factor the terms:
> $$
> a(d-c)=c(b-d)
> $$
> 
> Divide both sides by $(b-d)(d-c)$:
> $$
> \frac{a-c}{b-d}=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}
> $$


> [!NOTE] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> 2:5 = 6:15
> $$
>
> Apply Subtrahendo:
>
> $$
> \frac{2-6}{5-15}
> $$
>
> $$
> -4:-10 = 2:5
> $$


## Logarithm

### Basic 

The logarithm of a number to a given base is the **index (power)** to which the base must be raised to obtain the number.

If  
$$
a^{x}=n
$$
then  
$$
\log_{a} n = x
$$

**Example**
$$
3^{4}=81
$$
$$
\log_{3}81=4
$$

**Conditions for logarithm**
- Number must be positive  
- Base must be positive  
- Base cannot be equal to zero or  1  

$$
n>0,\quad a>0,\quad a\neq1
$$





> [!info] Why the base of a logarithm cannot be 1
> **Standard results**
> $$
> \log_a 1 = 0,\qquad \log_a a = 1
> $$
> 
> If base $=1$, then by definition:
> $$
> \log_1 x = y \;\Rightarrow\; 1^y = x
> $$
> 
> But
> $$
> 1^y = 1 \quad \text{for all } y
> $$
> 
> So:
> - $\log_1 1$ is **not unique**
> - $\log_1 x$ for $x \neq 1$ is **not possible**
> 
> Therefore, logarithms are **undefined** for base 1.
> 
> $$
> \boxed{a \neq 1}
> $$
> 




###  Law I - Product Rule


> The logarithm of the product of two numbers is equal to the sum of their logarithms, to the same base.  
> If $m,n>0$ and $a>0,\ a\neq1$, then

$$
\log_a(mn)=\log_a m+\log_a n
$$


> [!note] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> \log_2(8\times32)
> $$
>
> Using Product Rule:
>
> $$
> \log_2 8+\log_2 32
> $$
>
> $$
> 3+5=8
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \log_2(256)=8
> $$

### Law II — Quotient Rule

> The logarithm of the quotient of two numbers is equal to the difference of their logarithms, to the same base.  
> If $m,n>0$ and $a>0,\ a\neq1$, then

$$
\log_a\!\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)=\log_a m-\log_a n
$$


> [!note] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> \log_2\!\left(\frac{32}{8}\right)
> $$
>
> Using Quotient Rule:
>
> $$
> \log_2 32-\log_2 8
> $$
>
> $$
> 5-3=2
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \log_2 4=2
> $$

### Law III — Power Rule

> The logarithm of a number raised to a power is equal to the power multiplied by the logarithm of the number, to the same base.  
> If $m>0$ and $a>0,\ a\neq1$, then

$$
\log_a(m^n)=n\log_a m
$$


> [!note] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> \log_2(4^3)
> $$
>
> Using Power Rule:
>
> $$
> 3\log_2 4
> $$
>
> $$
> 3\times2=6
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \log_2 64=6
> $$

### Change of Base Theorem

> If the logarithm of a number to any base is given, then the logarithm of the same number to any other base can be determined using the change of base formula.  
> If $m>0$ and $a,b>0,\ a\neq1,\ b\neq1$, then

$$
\log_b m=\frac{\log m}{\log b}=\frac{\log_a m}{\log_a b}
$$

> A useful identity:
$$
\log_b a \times \log_a b = 1
$$


> [!note] Illustration
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> \log_{32} 64
> $$
>
> Apply Change of Base:
>
> $$
> \log_{32} 64=\frac{\log_2 64}{\log_2 32}
> $$
>
> $$
> =\frac{6}{5}
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \log_{32} 64=\frac{6}{5}
> $$

> **Note:** This method gives conceptual clarity,

>

#### Q6
If 
$\displaystyle \frac{x}{\,b+c-a\,}=\frac{y}{\,c+a-b\,}=\frac{z}{\,a+b-c\,}=k$,
then find the value of  
$(b-c)x+(c-a)y+(a-b)z$.

- (a) 1  
- (b) 0  
- (c) 5  
- (d) none of these  

> [!done]- Solution
>
> $$
> \frac{x}{b+c-a}=\frac{y}{c+a-b}=\frac{z}{a+b-c}=k
> $$
>
> $$
> x=k(b+c-a),\quad y=k(c+a-b),\quad z=k(a+b-c)
> $$
>
> $$
> (b-c)x+(c-a)y+(a-b)z
> $$
>
> $$
> =k\big[(b-c)(b+c-a)+(c-a)(c+a-b)+(a-b)(a+b-c)\big]
> $$
>
> $$
> =k\big[(b^2-c^2-ab+ac)+(c^2-a^2-bc+ab)+(a^2-b^2-ac+bc)\big]
> $$
>
> $$
> =k(0)
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}
> $$

#### Q77
If $A:B = 3:4$, $B:C = 7:9$, $C:D = 2:3$, and $D$ is 50% more than $E$, find the ratio between $A$ and $E$.

- (b) 7 : 12  
- (c) 3 : 5  
- (d) 4 : 5  

> [!tip]- Key Idea + Explanation (Chain Ratio Method)
> Given:
> $$
> A:B = 3:4,\quad B:C = 7:9,\quad C:D = 2:3
> $$
>
> Make $B$ same:
> $$
> A:B = 21:28,\quad B:C = 28:36
> $$
>
> Make $C$ same:
> $$
> C:D = 36:54
> $$
>
> Now relate $D$ and $E$:
> $$
> D \text{ is 50% more than } E \Rightarrow D = 1.5E
> $$
> $$
> \Rightarrow E = \frac{2}{3}D
> $$
>
> So:
> $$
> D:E = 3:2 \Rightarrow 54:36
> $$
>
> Therefore:
> $$
> A:E = 21:36 = 7:12
> $$
>
> ✅ **Answer: (b) 7 : 12**
