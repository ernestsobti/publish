## Definition
- A captive finance company is a wholly-owned subsidiary of an automaker or retailer that provides loans and other financial services to the customers of those companies.
- Captive finance companies provide store credit cards for retailers and full-scale banking, including multi-year auto loans.
- Their purpose is to provide the parent company with a substantial source of profit and also limit the company's risk exposure.

> [!Important] 
> One aspect of captive finance companies that may give investors pause: the companies will often offer shorter loan periods than other types of lenders, meaning monthly payments will be higher.


## Example of Captive Finance Company 

The best-known examples of captive finance companies are found in the automobile industry and the retail sector. When it comes to the auto sector, captive finance companies offer car loans to buyers in of need financing. Some examples include General Motors Acceptance Corporation, Toyota Financial Services, Ford Motor Credit Company, and American Honda Finance.

Notably, after the bankruptcy of General Motors in 2009, GMAC underwent a name change to [Ally Bank](https://www.investopedia.com/ally-invest-also-cuts-commissions-4772295) and rebranded as Ally Financial in 2010. Each company represents the financing and credit divisions of the larger brand name automobile manufacturer.
