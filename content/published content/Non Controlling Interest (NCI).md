Non-controlling interests are measured at the [[Net Asset Value]] of entities. They do not account for potential **voting rights**. There are two different types of non-controlling interest: direct and indirect. 

Non-controlling interest, also known as a minority interest, involves owning less than 50% of a company's shares and having no control over corporate decisions. In contrast, controlling interests give shareholders voting rights and influence over company strategy.
